Bradley Crohurst Roofing Sales Process
1.
Warm Entry & Rapport Building
Goal: Get the homeowner talking, relax defenses, and build connection.
Your Actions:
● Ask openers like “So what’s going on?” or “Tell me what’s been
happening with the roof.”
● As you walk through the home, look for points of connection: pets, family
photos, cars, hobbies, political stickers, military memorabilia, etc.
● Accept any offered drink or hospitality — this subtly builds trust.
● Keep the focus on them for the first few minutes — make them feel heard
and seen.
Purpose:
You’re not selling — you’re earning permission to sell later. You’re separating
yourself from “a roofer” and positioning as a trusted advisor.
2.
Establish Personal Credibility & Passion (“Your Why”)
Goal: Humanize yourself and share your authentic story.
Your Script Highlights:
● Share your background as a roofer and business owner who “wore every
hat.”
● Explain how you joined Arizona Roofers not because you needed a job,
but because you found shared values and better support.
● Mention meeting Anthony and being impressed by his integrity and
leadership.

● Explain that now, with the company’s structure and resources, you can
truly serve homeowners better.
Purpose:
This builds trust and enthusiasm — homeowners feel safer buying from
someone who’s clearly proud of their company and their craft.
3.
Company Story & Value
Goal: Show how Arizona Roofers is different — proof of quality and process.
Key Points:
● Veteran-owned and operated (discipline, consistency, honor).
● Ongoing training for roofers and sales staff — continuing education.
● Quality Control team inspects start and finish of every project.
● Most companies don’t have QC — they rely on subs and only react when
something leaks.
● Emphasize your workmanship warranty (different from manufacturer
warranty).
Purpose:
Shift focus from price to trust and process. You’re now selling confidence and
peace of mind.
4.
Set Expectations for the Inspection
Goal: Prepare them for what comes next while getting micro “yeses.”
Say something like:

“Now what I’m going to do is walk the perimeter, take photos
of anything that might concern us — fascia, patio lights, eaves,
all that. Then I’ll get up on the roof and check everything, take
plenty of pictures, and I’ll show you exactly what I find. Sound
good?”
Use “Does that sound good?” or “Would that be okay?” to get multiple
small affirmations.
Ask quick buy-in questions like what color drip edge they prefer — gets
them involved early.
5.
Roof Inspection & Documentation
Goal: Create irrefutable visual evidence and “the pain.”
Your Steps:
● Take 80–100+ photos (ground, fascia, eaves, valleys, penetrations,
damage).
● Look for “money shots” — close-ups of brittle underlayment, rot,
staining, slipped tiles, etc.
● Gather debris or brittle underlayment samples to show later.
Purpose:
The more clear your visual story, the easier the sale. Pain = motivation.
6.
Presentation & Pain Creation
Goal: Visually and emotionally demonstrate the problem.
Process:

● Sit at a table with your laptop or tablet.
● Show only the key photos (the worst ones).
● Use physical samples: let them touch the brittle paper, then the high-temp
synthetic.
● Use relatable phrases like:
“You wouldn’t know this unless you were a roofer — see
how this just turns to dust?”
● Use small bits of humor and empathy (“Don’t worry, most people don’t
crawl on their roof for fun.”)
Purpose:
You’re not telling them they need a new roof — you’re helping them realize it
themselves.
7.
Product & Warranty Presentation
Goal: Position options, educate, and lead them to the best choice.
Your Three-Tier Approach:
1. Hybrid Synthetic (Good) – Nail seal, hybrid protection, 10-year
workmanship.
2. Ice & Water (Better) – Bulletproof, 15-year workmanship, classic tech.
3. High Temp “Forever Roof” (Best) – 50-year material / 25-year
workmanship, top-tier system for Arizona heat.
Let them touch and compare samples.
Make the upgrade seem like a no-brainer (“For a little bit more, you get the
best protection available”).
Purpose:

Make it their decision — tactile engagement leads to ownership.
8.
Financing Conversation
Goal: Uncover affordability without pressure.
Use your “pregnant pause” perfectly here:
“Do you have a home project fund set aside for the roof, or
would you prefer to use someone else’s money?”
(Then silence.)
If they lean toward financing:
● Introduce GoodLeap 12-month same-as-cash or 15-year fixed.
● Explain advantages using your personal tone: “I like to keep a cushion
myself — why spend savings when you can leverage someone else’s
money?”
Purpose:
Smooth, pressure-free way to lead into payment conversation.
9.
Close
Goal: Transition naturally into signing — no surprises.
Flow:
● Review the chosen package (click “Details” on your proposal).
● Read the scope line-by-line, emphasizing transparency.
● Say:
“Our deposit is 50%. Would you like to do financing, a

check, ACH, or card?”
(Then pause again.)
Once they commit:
● Complete financing or collect deposit.
● Explain pre-lien notice calmly (“It’s standard in construction; it just
protects both sides”).
● Finalize color selections, confirm contact details, and set expectations for
next contact.
10.
Post-Sale & Production Handoff
Goal: Maintain trust and professionalism.
● Enter photos and details into CompanyCam immediately.
● Send production report before leaving.
● Text or call to thank them within 24 hours:
“Thanks again for trusting us with your home — you’re in
great hands with our production team.”
Key Psychology Principles You’re Already Using
● Mirroring: Matching tone and topics of interest to build connection.
● Authority: Sharing technical expertise and your “why” story.
● Scarcity & Exclusivity: Few companies have your warranties.
● Reciprocity: Taking water, complimenting home, building goodwill.
● Commitment & Consistency: Micro “yeses” build toward the final yes.
● Visual Anchoring: Using photos and samples creates tactile proof.
